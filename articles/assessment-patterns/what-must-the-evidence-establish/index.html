What Must the Evidence Establish?

A plausible explanation is a beginning. Establishing an answer means examining whether the other admissible explanations could change it.

Jonathan Nacionales · 8 min read

  • Evidence
  • Arrangements
  • Reasoning modules

On this page

  2. An unreadable code can conceal another fault
  3. The findings create reusable decision points
  4. The same structure can establish a coarser answer

Leah has one day to prepare 120 gift kits. A printed code will not scan, and the campaign’s recipient journey may be broken. Replacing every part could waste the correction budget. Reprinting only the insert could leave a wrong destination untouched. Before she compares responses, she needs a defensible answer to a smaller question: what do the recorded checks actually establish?

The first temptation is to settle on a plausible story. Perhaps the printer caused everything. Perhaps the link is wrong. Those stories suggest different work. Choosing the cheapest story would confuse what Leah wants to do with what the evidence supports.

This assessment pattern asks what follows from a fixed record under declared rules and assumptions. Its candidates represent possible interpretations of that record. The interpretation is something to examine, rather than an operating option Leah is free to choose.

Start with the claim that matters

Evidence is useful in relation to a question. “Something is wrong” may be enough to stop a handoff, but too vague to specify a correction. “The printed code needs replacing and the destination needs correcting” is more demanding. It requires distinguishing the relevant fault combinations, while leaving unrelated details open.

Begin with the observations, their scope and the rules linking a possible condition to an observable result. Construct the complete interpretations the model allows. Reject those that conflict with the admitted record. Then ask the same question in every interpretation that remains.

An answer is established when at least one admissible interpretation exists and a completed counterexample check shows that no admissible interpretation changes it. This is stronger than finding one explanation that agrees with the desired answer. It is also more precise than demanding a unique explanation of everything.

The structure of an evidence assessment
PartMeaning

An unreadable code can conceal another fault

The gifting example separates three possible faults: an unreadable printed code, a wrong destination and a restricted checkout. Its published model includes their joint combinations. It also specifies what the available checks would report in each combination. These are fictional teaching assumptions, not a universal account of every failed scan.

An unreadable printed code establishes a printing problem under this model. It does not reveal the destination concealed in that code. Both “printing problem only” and “printing problem plus wrong destination” can agree with the failed scan. The preferred correction cannot settle that ambiguity.

An illustrative sequence of scoped checks using the gifting model
Admitted recordWhat the modeled interpretations establish
The printed code is unreadableA print fault is established. The destination and checkout questions remain open.
The source artwork also shows the wrong targetA destination fault is established as well. Checkout still needs its own basis.
The intended account completes the scoped checkout checkUnder the published predictions, the supported correction is to replace the insert and correct the destination; a checkout repair is not established.

Scope is part of the meaning of each observation. The artwork revision, batch, test device and intended account matter. A successful checkout for another account cannot silently answer this account’s question. A report for an earlier revision retains that earlier scope; changing a label would manufacture evidence.

Follow Leah’s gift-kit correction case to see these findings feed a complete operating comparison. The evidence assessment establishes the correction need. The planning assessment separately checks which package can complete that work before handoff.

The findings create reusable decision points

The recurring structure includes what to consider after each finding. A business workflow specializes those response points with particular records, owners and available actions. It does not turn every open question into a requirement to investigate.

Calculation limits require their own reading. An interrupted search that has found no admissible interpretation has not proved inconsistency. Conversely, finding two admissible interpretations with different answers demonstrates ambiguity even if other interpretations remain unexplored. An established Boolean answer can be false; “false” is an answer, not an execution failure.

The same structure can establish a coarser answer

Consider a separate finite teaching example: one of nine marbles is heavier, and the weighing reports allow a declared bound on recording errors. Three heavy-marble identities remain compatible with the record. All three are in the red bag. The bag is established while the exact marble remains unresolved.

The gift-kit and marble domains use different entities and rules. They share the same reasoning question: do all admissible interpretations agree on the answer we need? This is how the pattern transfers. Matching the words “test” or “evidence” is insufficient; the candidate meaning and conclusion contract must also match.

Carry the conclusion with its limits

Logical compatibility supplies no probabilities. Three remaining explanations need not be equally likely, and counting them does not by itself justify entropy or an expected-value calculation. How Much Uncertainty Is Left? explains why measuring uncertainty needs a stated basis. The evidence assessment also does not authenticate observations, guarantee a minimal explanation of every conflict or authorize corrective work.

Use this pattern when the meaningful alternatives can be represented finitely and the interpretation rules are explicit enough to assess. When the model omits a real failure mode, unanimity within that model cannot establish a broader real-world claim. A conclusion needs its source basis, scope, assumptions and evaluation coverage alongside its answer.

From Arrangements to Assessments explains the shared foundation. Can We Complete the Commitment? develops the next judgment in Leah’s story: whether a complete correction plan meets the obligations. What Is a Question Worth? addresses the separate choice to obtain more information.

Leah can now separate a justified correction need from a convenient explanation. That gives production and finance a common basis for comparing responses, while leaving the work, fresh verification and actual handoff to the operating process.
