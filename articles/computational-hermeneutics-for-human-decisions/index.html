Computational Hermeneutics for Human Decisions

Examine what a statement or finding means before another part of a decision relies on it.

A workflow can bring several assessments into one decision. For that combination to be useful, the receiving step needs to know what each contribution establishes. A feasible schedule, evidence of completed work and permission to proceed support different conclusions. What may the next step rely on?

In Hermod Labs’ convention, workflows put decision patterns into practice and configured apps apply supporting reasoning patterns. Composing those parts requires attention to meaning: the information one part supplies must fit the question another part needs to answer.

Interpret meaning at the connection between parts

For our purposes, computational hermeneutics means using explicit representations and computational tools to help people examine what statements mean in context and what follows from particular readings. It makes interpretation part of the work of connecting information, assessments and commitments.

Shared structure gives us a way to reuse reasoning. Interpreting that structure in context helps us decide which reuse is justified. A familiar label such as “available,” “accepted” or “complete” still needs a meaning appropriate to the decision.

Distinguish reusing a method from relying on a finding

Two kinds of reuse require different examinations.
Kind of reuseWhat needs examination
Reuse a reasoning methodDoes the method fit the new question, domain concepts and requirements? The model and its assumptions need to be appropriate to that setting.
Use a finding in another decisionDoes this particular finding establish what the receiving step needs, within the required scope and conditions? Its evidence and outstanding requirements remain relevant.

The same feasibility approach can support Alex’s street-team deployment and Maya’s shipment review. Both examine complete arrangements against recorded requirements. The domain meanings still differ: a qualified staff member, an available freight route and permission to spend each need appropriate definitions. Reusing the method does not make a staffing permission transferable to a shipment.

Using a finding requires a more specific judgment about what the next question needs. A result can contain more information than that question requires while still supplying the relevant support. Its usefulness in that handoff does not make it interchangeable with every other result. Two models reaching the same recommendation in one case also need not share the same meanings, assumptions or rules.

Clarify the Friday trial statement

“We can run the trial on Friday if Quality signs off.”

A scheduling review might read this as expected availability. Purchasing might treat it as permission to commit. The trial needs an identity, Friday needs a date, and “signs off” needs a meaning in the relevant process. This manual worked exchange shows a person clarifying the claim before another part of the decision relies on it.

A manual worked exchange: clarifying the Friday trial statement
StepWhat a person examines or records
Source and contextAn Operations planner writes the statement above in a discussion of trial T17, proposed for Friday, 12 June 2026. The team needs a confirmed line slot and purchasing authorization before committing to the supplier.
Possible readings“Can” might report expected line availability, confirm a reservation, grant permission, or express several of these together. Keep those readings provisional.
Question and reason“Are you describing expected availability, a reservation, permission to proceed, or several of these? What must Quality sign off?” The distinction matters because the plan requires a confirmed slot and the supplier commitment requires authorization.
Human answer“I expect the line to be available for T17 on 12 June, provided Quality accepts the trial procedure. I have not reserved the line, and I am not authorizing a purchase.”
Representation revisionKeep the original statement and answer with their source. Record trial T17; date 12 June 2026; claim: Operations expects line availability; condition: Quality accepts the T17 procedure. Replace the provisional readings of a reservation or purchasing permission with this conditional expectation.
ReassessmentThe meaning of the capacity claim is clearer. A confirmed line slot is still missing; procedure acceptance and purchasing authorization remain unconfirmed in this example. The proposed trial date stays the same. This answer alone does not establish that the arrangement meets the stated requirements.

The clarification preserves a conditional expectation of availability. A confirmed reservation and purchasing permission remain outstanding. The proposed date is unchanged, but the larger decision now has a more precise account of what the statement contributes.

The person answering can also challenge the categories: “None of those quite captures it; I can release the line only if the customer order finishes early.” That introduces another dependency to examine. The representation and the questions must remain open to revision as the context becomes clearer.

Preserve what the finding establishes and applies to

A contribution needs its subject and revision, status, scope, conditions, assumptions and source. Where it concerns permission, the relevant authority and limits matter too. “The trial is planned,” “the trial is complete” and “the trial passed” establish different things. Completion may make evidence available; a satisfactory finding still needs its assessment criteria and conditions of use.

A finding may concern one material revision or one production process. A permission may cover a limited amount or period. “The current specification” depends on which specification was current when the statement was made. Those details need to travel with a contribution whenever they affect how it can be used.

Ontology engineering supplies concepts for an activity, report, finding, authorization and their relationships. Interpretation asks which claim a statement supports in its context. Reasoning modules examine how the resulting claims and evidence contribute to a question. Understanding the claim leaves its accuracy and supporting evidence to assess.

Ask whether a replacement supports the intended use

Suppose another review requires a successful trial finding for material revision 12. A feasible schedule for testing revision 12 leaves that requirement outstanding. A successful trial finding for revision 11 also leaves it outstanding. The first differs in what it establishes; the second differs in what it applies to. Neither supplies the required finding for revision 12.

Different wording can preserve a claim. “Maya reviewed report R” and “Report R was reviewed by Maya” can express the same relationship when the person, report and meaning of review are fixed. Replacing “reviewed” with “approved” changes what another step might conclude. A review can result in acceptance, rejection or a request for more evidence.

The Liskov substitution principle offers an engineering analogy: examine whether a replacement preserves what its user may rely on. We apply that question to a particular interpretive handoff; natural-language expressions are not thereby software subtypes.

Allow varied wording when it preserves the relevant claim and its conditions. Keep an uncertain reading provisional until the ambiguity that matters to the decision has been examined.

Interpret, represent, examine and clarify

The practical cycle is to propose an interpretation, represent it, inspect its consequences, seek clarification and revise. If a recorded statement is treated as a resource commitment, it may conflict with another activity. If it is a conditional expectation, a reservation may remain outstanding. The difference identifies a useful question to take back to the source.

A conflict can reflect a mistaken interpretation, an incorrect fact elsewhere in the model or a real disagreement. Computation helps expose the consequences of a reading. The speaker’s intended meaning still requires examination in context; choosing a convenient interpretation merely because it removes a conflict would weaken the review.

David Fuenmayor and Christoph Benzmüller’s computational-hermeneutics research examines formal interpretations of individual arguments alongside their role in a larger argument. This iterative movement between parts and whole is the hermeneutic circle. Using that idea to examine decision-workflow handoffs is Hermod’s engineering application of the approach.

Keep the original wording, its author and date, the relevant context, the clarification and the assumptions used to interpret it. Another person can then challenge the recorded reading without reconstructing the entire exchange. A faithful interpretation may reveal that the proposed action still lacks support.

Connect interpretation to the working review

In current Decision Lab authoring, people review how workflow questions and app results are connected. They record the intended meaning, scope and units of those mappings. The software checks the required declarations and review records against the current definitions. Those records retain a person’s stated judgment; they do not establish that the interpretation or underlying evidence is correct.

When an authored mapping changes, the affected authoring reviews need reconsideration. When a case fact changes within a published model, the relevant assessment and decision support need review. In both situations, explicit dependencies help connect the changed premise to the conclusions that relied on it. People clarify and model the meaning; configured apps and workflows calculate within those definitions.

In the shipment exception case, a consolidation confirmation for the previous order revision cannot establish permission for the current shipment. The case makes the distinction visible: preserve the record’s original scope, calculate again and inspect which arrangements remain supported. People still establish whether the source record is authentic and applicable.

Reusable reasoning becomes dependable when the connection preserves what a contribution establishes, where it applies and what remains unresolved. Computational hermeneutics gives that interpretive work an explicit place in the decision: a path from the source statement, through a reviewed representation, to the next step that relies on it.
