Article 7 traced Tomas's changed availability to a deadline conflict. Giving a tool the same job requires explicit descriptions of the choices, facts, and requirements involved.

The relationship maps have already given us a vocabulary: people, tasks, reports, and time windows. A phrase pattern is a sentence template with named slots and a defined meaning. Filling those slots names specific things and connects them. Every use of C must identify the same report.

Illustrative phrase patterns and filled examples for the review problem
Illustrative phrase patternFilled example
<report> is planned ready at <time>.C is planned ready at 11:00.
<person> is available from <start> to <end>.Tomas is available from 11:30 to 12:30.
Review of <report> takes <minutes> minutes.Review of C takes 60 minutes.
Review of <report> must finish by <time>.Review of C must finish by 12:00.

These patterns are illustrative. A tool needs a defined translation for each one. The 24-hour times share a workday and time zone. Draft readiness is assumed, availability is confirmed, and the deadline is required.

Every report needs one qualified reviewer who did not prepare it. A review runs uninterrupted for its duration, starts at or after the draft's ready time, fits the reviewer's availability, and finishes by its deadline. A person cannot overlap a review with another assigned task, including a meeting.

A candidate supplies choices: assign Tomas to review C starting at 11:30. The assignment becomes a labelled connection; its start and 60-minute duration place the bar in the schedule. Both views refer to the same proposed review:

Define the boundaries: by 12:00 includes 12:00. Consecutive tasks may share an endpoint without overlapping. These meanings determine which arrangements pass.

The bar fits the availability window and crosses the deadline: two relationships expressed in time. A full model also needs the other reports, reviewers, permissions, and approved meeting changes. Searching requires eligible choices and allowed times. Missing information needs a declared policy: does it block a decision or leave a candidate awaiting confirmation?

Keep sources and confirmation times with inputs, including Tomas's 10:30 update. A passing result applies to the declared model. Actual completion still needs evidence. Start with examples you can check by hand, then compare the tool's results with your reasoning.
