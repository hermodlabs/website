You need a report prepared and independently reviewed by noon. Maya is your strongest writer, so you ask her to prepare it. She has the time, knows the subject, and agrees to help. It looks like an easy decision.

Then you try to assign the review.

In this example, Maya and Noah are both qualified to prepare the report. Either can write it between 9 and 11 that morning. Maya and Priya are the only qualified reviewers. Review takes an hour, and the reviewer must be someone other than the person who prepared the report. Maya is available from 11 to noon. Priya cannot start until 1.

Once Maya writes the report, she cannot review it. Priya can review it, but she will miss the deadline. Choosing Maya for preparation has used the only person who could perform the review in time.

The combinations make the problem visible:

Preparation and review assignments and whether they satisfy the requirements
PreparesReviewsResult
MayaMayaFails the independent review requirement
MayaPriyaReview finishes after noon
NoahMayaPreparation and review finish by noon
NoahPriyaReview finishes after noon

Assigning preparation to Noah leaves Maya available to review his work. Both people are qualified for their assigned tasks, the review is independent, and the sequence fits the morning.

Follow the same tasks through both pictures. The relationship map shows who performs them and which report connects them. The schedule shows their duration and order. Together they explain why this arrangement works.

This is the arrangement problem. Which combinations of available choices can jointly satisfy the requirements? A candidate arrangement specifies the choices and how they connect. A workable arrangement satisfies the requirements when those choices are considered together.

The relationship between the choices matters as much as the choices themselves. You could give both tasks to qualified people and still miss the deadline. You could give both tasks to someone with enough time and still violate the independence requirement.

Before committing to one choice, examine which routes remain available for the work that depends on it.

Think about a decision you are making now. What must happen after your preferred choice? Who or what could do that work? Does the choice preserve a way to finish?
