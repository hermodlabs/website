Shared Structure for Automated Reasoning

Hermod’s convention connects decision patterns, supporting reasoning, workflows and apps while preserving what each finding establishes.

Hierarchical decomposition lets us examine manageable parts of a decision. Bringing those parts together requires shared meanings: what question each part answers, what its result establishes and when that result can support a commitment.

At Hermod Labs, we use a convention for organizing this work. We distinguish the recurring problem, the overall decision approach, its supporting reasoning and the software implementation. The convention gives authors a common structure while leaving the goals and acceptable tradeoffs with the people responsible for the decision.

The convention we use

Decision patterns describe reusable approaches to organizing a decision. Reasoning patterns describe reusable methods for answering its supporting questions. A decision pattern connects the work to a purpose; its supporting reasoning helps establish what can be concluded from the available information.

We put decision patterns into practice through workflows, using configured apps to apply supported reasoning patterns. A workflow coordinates questions, reviews, app assessments and the steps leading to a choice or commitment. An app supplies a domain model and the rules and meanings used in its assessment.

Hermod’s organizing convention, illustrated by a fictional street-team decision.
LevelMeaning in Sam’s example
Problem familyAllocate limited staff between outreach and customer service while preserving minimum floor coverage.
Decision patternCompare complete assignments, examine their requirements and exposure, then make a bounded operating commitment.
Supporting reasoning patternsCheck staffing, timing, access and spending conditions; calculate conditional expense-recovery thresholds; revisit findings when their supporting facts change.
ImplementationHermod’s authored workflow, configured operating app, financial calculations, questions, review responsibilities, reports, and separate choice and authorization steps.

The relationship resembles strategy and tactics: an overall approach draws on supporting methods. Here, that analogy concerns the roles of the patterns. Sam’s immediate staffing choice is an operating decision. Several combinations of supporting methods may serve the same overall decision purpose.

These are distinctions in our convention, rather than a fixed sequence of four software objects. A pattern describes an approach that can be adapted. Its implementation makes particular choices about questions, models, rules and handoffs.

From a reusable approach to a working review

In Sam’s fictional promotion decision, five staff are available and four must remain inside. Promoters want people on outreach; the floor team wants capacity for customer service. Sam wants to improve profit without making an unsupported commitment. Recognizing the shared-staff allocation problem helps us identify which alternatives and requirements the review must bring together.

The decision pattern is a bounded deployment review. The workflow gathers the operating facts, organizes their review, presents the findings and retains Sam’s separate choice and specific authorization. Its configured operating app checks complete assignments against the staffing, timing, access and spending requirements.

The workflow also calculates conditional guest thresholds from the assessed expense and reviewed financial assumptions. In this implementation, that calculation lives in the workflow. Apps provide reusable assessments, while workflows may contain supporting calculations and review logic as well as coordinating those assessments.

Hermod’s contribution was to recognize the recurring structure, select the supporting reasoning and turn it into an authored review. The resulting workflow makes those choices available for use and examination. It establishes no customer-response forecast: Sam still judges whether he is willing to risk $200 on the opportunity.

Preserve the different meanings of approval

The same convention applies to the supplier decision used elsewhere in this series. “The supplier is approved” could mean a satisfactory technical finding, accepted commercial terms or permission to place an order. Each meaning supports different next steps.

A technical finding identifies what was assessed and the conditions under which it applies. An authorization identifies the action it permits and the authority supporting it. A workflow can require both without collapsing them into a single approval status. Planned work also remains distinguishable from completed work.

Ontology engineering defines these concepts and relationships. Phrase patterns provide consistent ways to express statements using them. Reasoning modules organize the claims, evidence and rules around particular questions. Those shared meanings let one part interpret another part’s contribution.

Define the conditions software can check

Suppose a supplier’s qualifying trial needs exclusive use of a production line for two hours. A customer order also needs that line during part of the same period. Identifying the same resource, recording both intervals and declaring its capacity gives software a basis for checking the conflict. The check follows from the modeled conditions.

A purchasing review may also require a satisfactory technical finding and spending authorization. Recording one leaves the other outstanding. The software can check that distinction because the model preserves it. Someone must still establish that the records, requirements and their scope fit the actual purchase.

This is the practical value of a shared convention. Rails naming conventions provide a familiar analogy: connecting a model called “Book” to a table called “books” gives the framework a recurring mapping it can implement. In decision modeling, explicit concepts, relationships and rules give software defined consequences to calculate and check.

Compose reasoning without losing its meaning

A decision pattern can use several reasoning patterns, and the same reasoning pattern can support several decision patterns. A workflow may use multiple apps. Another workflow can reuse a suitable assessment component while organizing different questions, review responsibilities or commitments. Pattern languages help describe these connections and the reasons for choosing them.

Alternative methods need compatible contributions. Their inputs, units, scope, assumptions and outstanding conditions must establish what the receiving question requires. A feasible schedule cannot supply evidence that work has finished. Two findings based on one report retain that shared dependency; combining them does not create independent evidence.

Reuse also preserves the boundary between structure and a case. Another evening can use the same published review with newly reviewed facts. Changing its required coverage rule or introducing a requirement the model does not express needs an authored revision. An earlier conclusion or authorization retains the scope of the earlier situation.

When another employee calls out before Sam’s team leaves, the changed staffing returns the decision to Sam. The review retains the earlier choice while showing that its operating basis is no longer current. This connects a smaller factual change to the larger commitment that depended on it.

Keep the convention open to revision

Our convention concerns how reasoning is organized. The people responsible supply the goals, standards, evidence and acceptable tradeoffs. A completed assessment, a feasible arrangement, an established preference, a human choice and an authorization carry different meanings. The review must preserve the distinctions its decision needs.

The convention also needs examination through use. A missing distinction, an unsuitable module boundary or a handoff that loses scope gives us a reason to revise it. Changes to meanings and rules require attention to the dependent models and reviews.

Shared structure makes selected reasoning reusable and its modeled consequences available for automation. Workflows organize how those contributions reach a decision; apps provide configured assessments within that process. People can inspect the basis, question the assumptions and decide what commitment the findings support.
