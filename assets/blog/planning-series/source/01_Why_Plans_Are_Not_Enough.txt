Why Plans Are Not Enough
What a plan establishes, and what still needs a decision
Jonathan Nacionales
Founder, Hermod Labs
November 18, 2026
“Plans are worthless, but planning is everything.” Dwight D. Eisenhower used those words in November
1957 while explaining preparation for emergencies. Events would differ from the anticipated situation;
the understanding developed through planning would help people respond intelligently. He introduced
the phrase as a saying he had heard in the Army.[1]
That observation gives us a practical business question: what should planning preserve beyond the
instructions in a plan? A proposal can name every person, service, and date while leaving its feasibility,
comparative value, or acceptance unresolved. Those are different claims.
We can expose them with a short decision statement. Each named slot identifies information to supply, a
conclusion to establish, or a decision to record:
This plan includes plan_contents.
We have checked it under these conditions: assessed_conditions.
The result establishes established_finding.
We favor preferred_options because preference_basis.
Acceptance of next_action is acceptance_status, with decision_owner responsible.
If we observe reassessment_trigger, we will contingency_response before response_deadline.
Some entries describe choices and assumptions. Others report assessment results or human decisions.
“Not established” and “pending” can be correct entries. Completing the wording is not proof that the
proposed work can proceed.
What does the shipment proposal include?
Consider a fictional supplier preparing a retailer’s product launch. Maya coordinates its shipment
recommendation. Launch wants the goods early, finance has set a $5,000 expense limit, and operations
needs records and resources that support the actual shipment. A revised purchase order has changed the
document specifying the goods and delivery requirements.
Maya has three quoted services. The comparison rule prefers the earliest arrival among arrangements
that meet all requirements.[2]
Service Expense Quoted arrival Current issue
Direct $4,000 Friday, noon Applicable supporting records are supplied.
Consolidated $5,000 Thursday, 6 p.m. Loading confirmation names the earlier order revision.
Backup $6,000 Thursday, noon Expense exceeds the $5,000 limit.
A loading confirmation identifies the reserved loading opportunity and the order it covers. A confirmation
for the previous revision does not establish the required support for the revised order.

Completeness asks whether the required choices have been resolved. A complete shipment
plan must connect the service to preparation, people, resources, and timing. We call this combination an
arrangement; it supplies plan_contents. “Someone will prepare the goods” leaves a responsibility open.
Naming a person and a time resolves the choice, even if that person later turns out to be unavailable.
Completeness is relative to the commitment being modeled. If the job includes preparation, transport,
and receipt, selecting a carrier alone cannot cover it. An assessment uses a model: the specified choices,
facts, and rules it will examine. An obligation omitted from that description remains outside the result.
Can the proposed choices work together?
Feasibility asks whether the choices jointly satisfy the applicable requirements under the
supplied conditions. An earlier arrival does not compensate for exceeding a mandatory expense limit.
A document’s presence does not make its scope current.
Under Maya’s supplied records, Direct passes. Backup is too expensive and Consolidated lacks the
required confirmation for the current order. A specific, complete plan that has passed the necessary
checks provides a feasible witness: concrete support that at least one arrangement meets the modeled
requirements. The established_finding should remain attached to the assessed_conditions that support
it.
This is a bounded conclusion. People still need to judge whether the model fits the operation and whether
the records are trustworthy. Feasibility under stated inputs is not an unconditional forecast of successful
delivery.
Which possible conditions did we examine?
A contingency is a possible condition or event that could affect the next action. To illustrate coverage,
extend the shipment situation with three uncertain conditions: another order change, loss of the loading
window, and transit taking one day longer than quoted. If all combinations are admitted, there are eight
modeled futures. The field names are order_change, loading_window_status, and transit_duration.
Figure 1 highlights two futures examined by a hypothetical narrow review. These branches concern
conditions Maya may face; they are not service choices she can select. Choosing a carrier does not choose
whether the customer changes the order again.
Even if a plan passes both examined futures, the other six remain unresolved. They are not thereby
impossible or failed. Two of eight examined scenarios is also not a 25 percent success probability.
Examining all eight would answer the chosen model’s questions. It would not show that the real operation
contains only those three relevant conditions. A new receiving restriction or an unavailable worker could
require a different model. This is a practical argument for continuing to plan, not a theorem that every
plan must fail.
Information entropy can quantify uncertainty over a specified set of outcomes when their probabilities are
justified. It does not establish that the set is exhaustive. A robust arrangement may succeed across many
modeled futures while their occurrence remains uncertain. Examining vulnerabilities across plausible
futures is central to robust decision making.[6]

Why favor one feasible arrangement?
Maya receives a separately supplied confirmation covering the revised order. With the other conditions
satisfied, Consolidated now passes. Direct remains feasible. The arrival-first rule prefers Consolidated’s
Thursday delivery for $5,000 over Direct’s Friday delivery for $4,000. Backup remains over budget.[3]
Established preference supports a comparison under explicit criteria. The preferred_options
now names Consolidated; preference_basis records the priority and supporting information. Finance
can inspect why the additional $1,000 is justified by that priority. Different priorities could support a
different preference.
Several alternatives may also tie under the declared rule. Keeping all preferred ties is a valid result. A tie
differs from a comparison that remains unresolved; silently displaying one tied option as the sole winner
invents a distinction.
Completion of a command, assessment, or report remains a separate issue:
Assessment outcome Complete? Witness? Preference?
All required evaluation finishes; no plan passes Yes No No
Limited search finds a fully checked passing plan No Yes No
Complete comparison establishes a valid preferred tie Yes Yes Yes
Finding a witness need not require examining every candidate. Establishing that none works, or that
one is best, needs the relevant coverage or another sound proof or bound. Input diagnostics, unavailable
calculations, and completed blocked findings therefore call for different responses. A fully retained report
is not itself proof of feasibility or preference.
Who has agreed to the next action?
Human acceptance records a responsible person’s decision about a particular action and
proposal. Existing permissions may already be modeled requirements. The separate question is whether
the responsible person has accepted this next commitment under the reviewed conditions.

For example, next_action could be “reserve Consolidated for the revised order.” Its acceptance_status
mayremainpendingwhiletheauthorizeddecision_owner reviewsit. Ausefulrecordpreservestheproposal,
reviewed basis, person, action, and conditions. Acceptance does not establish that the reservation or

The same distinctions apply to a second fictional operation. Leah coordinates 120 gift kits whose printed
code must lead recipients to a usable online checkout. Within a $600 correction limit, an in-house package
costs $300 and finishes at 40 minutes. An external package costs $400 and finishes at 25 minutes, but its
confirmation initially names an old insert version. A current confirmation makes the external package
preferred under the earlier-handoff priority; the in-house package remains feasible.[5]
Another campaign has an hour before its cutoff, yet its recorded availability, permissions, and confirmation
have expired. A completed review finds no passing package. Time remaining alone cannot supply the
missing operating support. [4]
Put the distinctions to work
Decision Lab represents alternatives, checks their modeled requirements, and compares passing arrange-
ments under specified preference rules. The four linked shipment and gifting guides show repeated
reviews through a command-line interface (CLI) and individual reviews in applications using a software
development kit (SDK). They retain calculated findings while leaving source trust, human acceptance,
and execution with the operating process.
The public examples distinguish assessment completion, feasible witnesses, established preferences, limited
searches, and genuine ties. They do not compute the entropy estimates developed in the next article.
Their independent shipment and campaign cases also do not establish a feasible allocation of shared
trucks, workers, or budgets.
Return to the opening statement before acting. What supports each entry? Which change reopens it?
Does the proposed response have the time, resources, and authority it needs? Those questions give the
team a basis for continuing when the situation changes.
