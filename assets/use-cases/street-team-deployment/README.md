# Street-team deployment: sources and scope

This fictional case follows Sam, a gentlemen’s-club manager, through an urgent promotion decision, a repeatable team review and a proposed learning plan. The three business articles explain who supplies the facts, how the team supports a decision and when changed facts require reconsideration. Implementation details are an optional repository reference. The case is based on the user-supplied outline, not on records from the user’s contact or an actual club.

## Decision map

`decision-map.json` is the versioned explanatory semantic model behind two separate article illustrations: the roles and decision authority, and the directional business trade-offs. It distinguishes declared stakeholder goals and reporting relationships from the existing recorded staff inputs, the `staffing-coverage` constraint under `SUFFICIENCY`, and retained coverage findings. Stable node identifiers and typed relationships describe the shared staff pool, overlapping activities, absence interval, floor minimum, alternatives and assessments.

Version 1.2.0 keeps the reporting hierarchy explicit: the floor team and promoters report to Sam, and Sam reports to the owner. Sam's objective is to improve profit; the teams prioritize people on the floor and people doing outreach. Those interests influence Sam, who alone makes the deployment decision within his delegated operating and spending scope. Reporting to the owner does not add an owner approval step. These author-declared relationships imply no authenticated identities or multi-user governance feature.

Two business-effect lanes connect those interests to Sam's objective. Floor staffing supports customer-service capacity, which may help retain sales. Outreach uses floor capacity and adds expense, but may attract additional guests. Attendance, guest spending and offer use affect revenue; revenue supports profit while promotion and other relevant costs reduce it. Five qualitative outcome nodes and nine typed relationships preserve the direction, basis and uncertain status of these effects. No coefficients, response probabilities or measured effects are supplied. Guest mix refers to spending, offer use and service needs, without inferred demographic effects.

The financial map is explanatory. Revenue, retained contribution and total venue profit have separate meanings. The existing financial comparison uses contribution after the offer and relevant variable costs, with any separate discount loss counted once. Its conditional thresholds do not forecast attendance, service quality, revenue or profit; possible sales lost through reduced service would require evidence and further modeling.

## Original editorial reference

`worked-case.json` is the original common editorial fixture, retained with its original `proposed-configuration` status and an explicit `historical-editorial-reference` role. That status describes this earlier design artifact, not the current implementation. It uses whole USD values for teaching; it is not a native app package or runtime case. It preserves four mutually exclusive options: no further push, one promoter at $200 with $40 contribution per additional guest, two at $400/$40, and two with a deeper discount at $400/$25. Five, ten and sixteen additional guests respectively cover those deployment costs. Six, eleven and seventeen produce the first positive results in these exact integer examples.

Seven staff are initially available; four must stay inside. Everyone counted toward that simplified requirement can do the required floor work throughout the absence. Two callouts leave five available: one deployed leaves four inside, whereas two deployed leaves three. A real model needs qualified roles and overlapping intervals.

The same-night local schedule is 7:30 p.m. review, 7:45 authorization and dispatch, 8 p.m. arrival at the promotion point, 8:15 event exit, 8:45 contact-window end, 9 p.m. staff return and 9:15 latest useful customer arrival. A planned 30-minute customer journey is conditional on acceptance. Moving the event exit to 9 p.m. misses the existing contact window and yields a 9:30 arrival, after the declared cutoff.

Staff callouts, lower standard-offer contribution and the late event are separate one-field scenarios against the baseline. They are not silently combined. Location access and the other declared conditions stay fixed. The no-push option remains a baseline, not an automatic winner or a prediction about the evening.

Contribution is venue-retained income after the offer and relevant variable costs. Deployment cost includes all additional spending caused by the choice. A separate loss on customers who would have attended anyway must be counted once. At $400 cost, $40 contribution and $50 such loss, twelve whole additional guests cover the combined amount. No customer count, response distribution, causal effect or profit-maximizing preference is supplied.
