Why repeatable symptoms hide

“Repeatable” doesn’t mean “obvious.” Symptoms repeat in ways dashboards aren’t built to detect.

Pattern

They’re local

One rack bay fails while everything else stays fine. The sensor is elsewhere, so the average stays green.

Pattern

They’re vertical

The pocket is a layer in canopy depth. Wall-height measurements miss the layer.

Pattern

They’re time-locked to triggers

The symptom happens in a window (post lights-off, post door, HVAC edge). Dashboards smooth over time instead of aligning to events.

Pattern

They’re geometry-dependent

Behind racks, under benches, between jets. A “representative sensor” can’t represent a trap.

The fix is not “more averages.” The fix is targeting: zone + trigger + window, on comparable cycles.

Can you exchange air through canopy depth where pockets form?

P

Proof loop

Can you run one-knob tests on comparable cycles and call a verdict?

FAQ

What if I don’t know the trigger?

That’s common. The assessment can route you to an observability/baseline-first pilot that helps identify triggers and windows.

Do I need new sensors?

Not necessarily. Most teams just need one measurement placed where the symptom lives (in-zone, canopy depth) and aligned to a trigger window.

Is this a score?

No. It’s a readiness classification so you don’t waste time on pilots your facility can’t support yet.
