Repair a Disrupted Commitment

What can we still deliver when capacity disappears?

Compare a missing cook with a QA shortfall to see how Decision Lab assesses complete repairs, and what changes between the two applications.

  • Restaurant staffing
  • Software delivery

Jonathan Nacionales · 7 min read

A cook becomes unavailable before the weekend. A software team discovers that its remaining quality assurance work exceeds its qualified capacity. Both teams have already made a promise. Both need an answer to the same immediate question: what can we still deliver?

The Repair a Disrupted Commitment pattern gives these situations a common starting point: retain the original obligation, supply complete recovery responses, and examine their consequences. These two fictional worked examples show how that approach changes when the work, resources and responsibilities change.

Keep the original promise in the comparison.

For restaurant manager Elena, the obligation is to cover the nine remaining roster positions across Saturday and Sunday. Nora cannot work Saturday’s 6–10 p.m. cooking shift. Moving another employee into that position may restore Saturday service while opening a gap on Sunday.

For release owner Nina, the obligation is to deliver core functionality and reporting by the original 5 p.m. cutoff. Remaining QA needs twelve hours for core and eight for reporting. Existing qualified capacity supplies sixteen hours. Deferring reporting reduces the workload and changes what the customer will receive.

In both applications, Decision Lab compares the supplied complete responses against the obligations and current records. The domain determines which conditions each response must satisfy.

Restaurant staffing: follow every connected move.

The restaurant application supplies four complete repairs. With relief cook Rowan available until 10 p.m., two meet the requirements: relief coverage and a coordinated repair using existing staff.

The coordinated response moves Theo to Saturday cooking. Ben covers Sunday cooking, and Ava covers Ben’s Sunday serving position. The other six positions stay unchanged. Each candidate is checked across the remaining roster for coverage, qualifications, full-interval availability, overlapping assignments, declared rest and hours, and remaining payroll.

The useful output is a complete proposal Elena can discuss with the team. This feasibility comparison establishes no preferred repair. Choosing between the passing responses can bring a further question about payroll or preserving published assignments.

Software delivery: additional capacity must fit the work.

The release application also supplies four responses. Full delivery with existing QA is blocked. Core-only delivery needs an accepted scope revision. Delivery on a later date needs an accepted deadline revision.

The remaining response adds a qualified four-hour independent regression package for $400, within the recorded $500 limit. It preserves both core and reporting, includes the one-hour release handoff, and reaches the original cutoff exactly. There is no modeled deadline slack.

The assessment establishes feasibility without ranking. Nina supplies the business preference for preserving scope and date within the expense boundary. She can explain why the response supports her proposal and where its vulnerability lies: any additional work or loss of available time needs reassessment.

Change one fact and examine what stops working.

Shorten Rowan’s availability from 10 p.m. to 8 p.m. The relief repair fails because the cooking assignment still runs until 10. The coordinated repair remains feasible. The obligation to cover dinner has stayed fixed; the available capacity has changed.

Keep Nina’s $400 quote and four-hour package fixed, but change the helper’s qualification to another build. The additional-help response becomes blocked. Nominal hours cannot establish qualification for the required release, validation environment and assignment interval.

These variations expose the transferable question: what makes this resource usable for this complete response? In the restaurant it includes covering the whole shift. In the release it includes qualification for the actual build and the ability to take the specified work. The applications give that question different records and checks.

A proposed scope or deadline change has another meaning again: it changes the promise. Nina needs the relevant acceptance recorded before relying on a revised release. Inspecting a calculated option does not supply that agreement.

Reuse the reasoning and adapt the application.

Three kinds of reuse are involved. The decision pattern organizes the recovery question. The implemented commitment assessment establishes which declared arrangements meet modeled obligations. An authored application supplies the domain’s inputs, candidate structure, requirements and report language.

A shorter availability window or a changed qualification can be assessed within these applications’ existing structures. A different service layout, employee catalog, set of transferable release tasks or dependency structure can require changes to the authored application. The restaurant example’s roster structure requires an adapted author profile and a reviewed release when those structural definitions change.

This is the practical value of the shared pattern: it helps identify what the next application must represent. Restaurant qualifications, release qualifications, service positions and validation tasks each need their own meanings. Their relationships determine whether a complete recovery response works.

Bring the comparison to your own work.

Start with a commitment whose operating basis has changed. Before adapting an application, answer these questions:

  • Which original obligations remain in force, and who can accept a revision?
  • What complete recovery responses can you supply, including effects on other work?
  • Which records establish qualifications, availability, prerequisites and permission?
  • Which changed fact should change a finding, and which structural change needs an adapted model?

That gives you a concrete basis for discussing a Decision Lab application. The resulting assessment can support a recovery proposal. Confirming staff, securing an agreement and communicating the chosen operating plan remain work for the responsible people.
