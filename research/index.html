Research

Make the reasoning visible.

Our tools put strategy engineering into practice. Our research asks whether making requirements, dependencies, assumptions, and alternatives explicit helps people understand and revise a plan.

Questions guiding the work

What should we
be able to show?

Can people inspect the reasons?

Can a person identify the assumptions and supporting relationships behind a finding, including where information is incomplete?

Can they compare alternatives?

Does the explanation make it clear what changes between options, what remains the same, and which tradeoffs still require judgment?

Can they trace the effect of a change?

When an input or arrangement changes, can people identify which conclusions and follow-up work deserve another look?

What transfers between applications?

Which reasoning structures are reusable across domains, and which depend on the vocabulary, models, and workflows of a particular application?

Evaluation approach

Start with a concrete task.
Keep the claims testable.

These principles guide our planned evaluations: define a question, make a fair comparison, and document what the results can and cannot tell us.

01

Define what understanding means

Give participants a specific plan and question. Look for their ability to explain a dependency, identify an unsupported assumption, or name a consequence of changing an option.

Evaluate an observable task.

02

Make the comparison explicit

Use the same decision context to examine different ways of presenting the reasoning. Document the information available in each comparison and where the comparison has limits.

Explain what was actually compared.

03

Look for failures as well as benefits

Examine where a model leaves something out, an explanation confuses a reader, or a change fails to prompt the right review. Use those observations to revise the approach.

Treat limitations as part of the findings.
