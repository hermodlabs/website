01 / The work behind a conclusion

Make the criteria clear.
Keep the questions visible.

Decide which test to run next, whether to repair equipment, or how to improve a procedure. A useful review makes the desired outcome, available evidence, and remaining uncertainty explicit.

01

Define what improvement means.

Make the objectives, constraints, and decision criteria explicit. Clarify whose work or outcomes a choice affects and what would make the result better for them.

Know what the decision is for.

02

Inspect the supporting evidence.

Connect a claim to the observations that support it. Keep the scope, assumptions, and unanswered questions visible enough to examine and challenge.

See what supports the conclusion.

03

Keep judgment open to revision.

Record the basis for a decision and the conditions it depends on. Make it possible to ask whether a conclusion still holds when new information arrives.

Revisit the reasons behind a choice.

Team

Seeking pilots

For people working together

Give each contribution a clear owner.

Bring evidence, reviews, and decisions into a shared record. Each reviewer records their own approval against the revision they examined, so everyone can see who decided what and on which basis.

  • Shared cases
  • Assigned reviews
  • Reviewer-authored approvals tied to specific revisions
  • A common history of contributions and decisions
