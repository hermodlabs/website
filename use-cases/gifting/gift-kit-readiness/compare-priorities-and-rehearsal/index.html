A cheap partial fix is still a partial fix.

These are the five supplied packages. The review does not assemble an unquoted combination of partial repairs; if the team can obtain a different combined package, it needs to be added and checked as another option.

An arrangement is a possible combination of a correction package, people, resources, timing and permissions that fits the rules and current context. A plan proposes one such combination. Here the internal arrangement needs 25 + 5 + 5 = 35 minutes; the confirmed external arrangement needs 10 + 5 + 5 = 20 minutes.

These calculations use the supplied start at minute 0. As the review progresses, Leah needs to refresh the current time, planned start and availability records before committing; the deadline stays at minute 1,440.

The example reuses a pattern of review the evidence, compare complete options, prepare a decision brief. Each stage passes its reviewed result to the next, so Leah does not have to manually translate the established faults into a separate planning exercise.

Finance can see that internal work costs less and meets the deadline. Marketing can see what buying 15 minutes would cost. The model supplies no monetary value for those minutes, so it does not establish that the extra $100 is worthwhile. The business owners need to explain why earlier handoff matters.

Both arrangements finish well within the one-day window. Earlier handoff is not needed simply to meet that deadline; the team needs a separate operational reason to choose it. The extra time gives Leah room to check the records and coordinate the work instead of treating every issue as an emergency.

Eligible means an arrangement meets the recorded requirements. Preferred means it ranks first under the chosen priority. Approved and completed requires people to authorize and perform the work; a preferred arrangement does not establish either.

What if the external work is not confirmed?

The collection’s starter has no external confirmation. A quote gives Leah an expense and duration; it does not establish that the external correction is available for these kits when needed.

Without that record, the external arrangement is blocked even in the earliest-handoff review. Internal replacement and repair remains eligible and is preferred at $300, with handoff at minute 35. The priority cannot make the missing condition disappear.

{% set figureKey = 'missingConfirmation' %}{% include "body/gifting-figure.html" %}

A usable confirmation must be positive, refer to the exact package and all six scope details, identify its source and recorder, be recorded by the current review time, and cover the correction through handoff. A record for another batch, an expired booking or information not yet available to Leah does not qualify.

Confirmation does not grant operating permission or create production capacity. Those remain separate requirements, alongside the budget limit. People still authenticate the source before relying on the record.

Give Leah a proposal she can stand behind.

In the starter review, Leah puts forward internal replacement and journey repair at $300, with handoff at minute 35. It addresses both established faults, fits the $600 limit and leaves time before the next day’s deadline. The external package still needs a valid confirmation.

Finance has a costed proposal to review. Production has the complete correction, fresh rehearsal and handoff sequence to inspect. Marketing can see how the plan addresses the recipient experience as well as the delivery promise. Leah can explain why the $100 reprint is insufficient and what would have to change before she proposes external work.

In the confirmed teaching comparison, the additional $100 buys a handoff 15 minutes earlier. Meeting the recorded deadline alone supports either complete arrangement, so Leah proposes the cheaper complete correction. The earliest-handoff review exposes the consequence of a different priority; it does not establish that the premium benefits this campaign.
